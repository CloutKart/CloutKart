# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

D2C brand founders, performance marketers, and growth-focused marketing managers whose creative production is the bottleneck on their ad spend. They are results-driven, budget-conscious, and skeptical of agencies that over-promise. Their pain is operational before it is aesthetic: too many handoffs, too much of their own time spent in the middle of the process, and no way to scale volume without scaling headcount. They know their numbers (ROAS, CTR, CAC) and make decisions based on them.

## Product Purpose

CloutKart **automates and optimises the creative production process** for D2C brands. It runs the pipeline end to end as six stations: research, winning message, format map, concepting, polish & QC, and export & deliver. Four run unattended; two are gates where a person approves (the message, then the finished batch). Pixie is the engine that drafts a run so the automated stations downstream have something unambiguous to build from.

The line is a **closed circuit**, not a sequence: campaign signal returns after launch and briefs the next run, so each batch starts from what converted rather than from memory. The site states this in one drawing (Sheet 02, "The Assembly") rather than in prose.

Success looks like a brand going from "creative is what holds up our spend" to a line that runs on a schedule, with the founder's involvement reduced to two approvals per run.

## Brand Personality

Aggressive · Precise · Craft

Like a rally car driven by an art director. High-performance, conversion-obsessed, but with genuine craft underneath. Not a generic SaaS. Not a polite agency. Direct-response energy with a quality finish.

Emotional goal for visitors: *confidence that this will actually work*, followed by urgency to start.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS purple — soft gradients, clean Notion-style white space, minimal, polite. The default AI landing page look (which the current site partially resembles). Avoid anything that feels like a wireframe with a purple tint.

Also avoid: corporate agency (soulless blue/white), over-designed experimental portfolio (art for art's sake — this is a performance tool), Web3/crypto maximalism.

## Design Principles

1. **Practise what you preach.** The site IS the product's argument. A page about automating a production process must look like the plan for one: legible, measured, drawn. If the page is vague about how the work happens, the offering is not credible.
2. **Show the mechanism, then the result.** Diagrams, station splits, and cycle times are the design elements that build trust, not decoration around copy. Every number on the page must already be a claim the business stands behind; derived or invented figures are worse than none.
3. **No filler.** Every section, every element earns its place. Like a good ad, nothing decorates for decoration's sake.
4. **Craft is visible.** The quality of the site signals the quality of the creative output. Precision in typography, motion, and spacing is evidence of craft, not just aesthetics.
5. **Message first.** Still the core doctrine, and now the input the whole run is built from: identify the one thing visitors need to understand at each point, and make that one thing unmissable.

6. **Graphite decides, violet executes.** The site's one semantic rule. Anything violet is automated; anything graphite is where a person is involved. It holds in every diagram, badge, and rail on the page, which is what lets a visitor read the automated share of the line before reading a word.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for all animations — crossfade or instant transition as fallback. Color contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for body text, ≥ 3:1 for large text. All interactive elements keyboard-navigable and ≥ 44×44px touch target.
